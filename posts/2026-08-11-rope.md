Attention has no built-in sense of order: shuffle the tokens and the outputs
shuffle with them. If a model is to know that "dog bites man" differs from
"man bites dog", you have to arrange it. Almost every open model now arranges
it with **rotary position embedding**, which adds nothing to the token vector
— it rotates it, by an angle proportional to position, chosen so the absolute
angles cancel when two tokens are compared.

[TOC]

## A Rotation That Is Thrown Away

In its original formulation RoPE is applied **inside each attention head**, to
queries and keys only, just before the dot product. It never touches the
values or the residual stream: the rotation happens to a copy, is used once,
and is gone — unlike an additive tag, which travels through the whole network.
"Original formulation" does real work there: GPT-J rotates a quarter of each
head's dimensions, DeepSeek's latent attention a dedicated subset, Llama 4
three layers in four. RoPE also has **no learned parameters**, and section 3
is how that eroded.

The 2021 question is the sharp one: is there an $f$ writing *absolute*
position into a vector such that the dot product depends only on the
*relative* one?

**Adoption, stated carefully.** Among dense open-weight decoder-only models
since 2023 RoPE is close to universal — but not universal: BLOOM and MPT use
ALiBi, Jamba nothing, and Mamba and RWKV are not attention models.

## 2. The Angle That Cancels

Start with two dimensions. With $R(\alpha)$ a rotation and $q = W_q x_m$,
$k = W_k x_n$ the projected query and key, RoPE sets $q_m = R(m\theta)q$ and
$k_n = R(n\theta)k$. Take the dot product, using the two facts that make
rotations special — $R(\alpha)^\top = R(-\alpha)$, and that they compose by
adding angles:

$$
q_m^\top k_n
= q^\top R(-m\theta)R(n\theta)\,k
= q^\top R\big((n-m)\theta\big)\,k .
$$

**The absolute positions have cancelled**, exactly, by the group structure of
rotation.

Where does the rotation come from? Read the pair as a complex number with a
magnitude and a phase: for the inner product to depend only on $n-m$, the
phase forces $\Theta(m) = m\theta$ and the magnitude forces $R_q(m)R_k(n)$
constant; set both radii to 1 and you have a rotation. A derivation, not a
uniqueness theorem — the condition pins down only the *product* of the radii,
and **xPos** takes that freedom.

A head is 64 or 128 dimensional, so RoPE chops the vector into consecutive
pairs and rotates each at its own rate, $\theta_i = \text{base}^{-2i/d}$, with
$\text{base} = 10{,}000$ originally. Pair $0$ turns one radian per token; each
subsequent pair turns geometrically slower, and the cancellation holds pair by
pair.[^sincos] RoPE is not one clock but a bank of them.

One practical note, probably a larger part of RoPE's victory than the elegance
above: it is $O(d)$ elementwise work leaving nothing inside the attention
matrix, so it composes with FlashAttention and a KV cache
untouched.[^pairing]

## 3. Stretching a Context It Was Never Given

A model trained with a 4,096-token context has seen every angle each band
produces inside it and none beyond. The fast bands are unbothered by position
100,000 — they wrap constantly. The slow ones are the problem: never having
completed a turn, they are put somewhere genuinely new.

    note.textContent = never + ' of the 64 bands have not completed a single turn at ' +
      'position ' + m.toLocaleString() + '. The slowest needs ' +
      Math.round(2 * Math.PI / slowest).toLocaleString() + ' tokens to come back round.' +
      (m === 4096 ? '  \u2014 this is Llama 2\u2019s training window.' : '');
  }
  pR.addEventListener('input', draw);
  bR.addEventListener('input', draw);
  draw();
})();
</script>

Every extension method answers that picture. **Position interpolation** (Chen
et al., 2023) is the blunt one: halve every position index so everything lands
back inside the trained range — with a thousand steps of fine-tuning, usually
dropped when the method is described. **NTK-aware scaling** changes the base
instead, stretching slow bands more than fast; it appeared in June 2023 as a
forum post by a pseudonymous user, bloc97, and was in production before any
paper. **NTK-by-parts** is the three-regime split Figure 4 shades, **YaRN**
adds a temperature on the logits, and **LongRoPE** searches per-band factors
out to 2M tokens. Choosing the base up front differs again: Llama 3 pretrained
with 500,000.

All of it asks *which clocks to slow down* — a coherent question only because
RoPE built position out of independent frequencies.

## 4. A Bound That Falls, and Nothing Underneath It

The paper's second argument got repeated everywhere: setting
$\theta_i = \text{base}^{-2i/d}$ gives "a long-term decay property [...],
which means the inner-product will decay when the relative position
increase."

<div class='figure narrow'>
    <img src='/images/rope-long-term-decay.png'
         alt='A decaying, heavily oscillating curve of a relative upper bound against relative distance, falling from about 20 to roughly 7 over 250 tokens.'>
    <div class='caption'>
        <span class='caption-label'>Figure 5.</span>
            The paper's evidence. Read the axis label: this is an <i>upper bound</i>
        on the inner product, not the inner product. It falls from about 20 to
        7 across 250 tokens, oscillating the whole way.
        <br>

A bound that falls obliges nothing underneath to fall. For $q, k$ with
independent standard-normal entries the cross terms vanish and the Pythagorean
identity leaves

$$
\operatorname{Var}\big(q_m^\top k_n\big) = 2 \cdot \tfrac{d}{2} = d
$$

for **every** relative distance: the typical logit does not decay at all.

    <img src='/images/rope-decay-test.png'

    <div class='caption'>

            Both quantities over the range the paper plots, each divided by its
    value at distance 10. The bound (lavender) falls to 0.37; the realised
    logit magnitude for random $q, k$ (blue) sits at 0.98 — flat, as the
    variance says it must. Decay is not imposed; it is something a model can
    arrange.

Barbero and colleagues reached the same place in 2024: "A common belief is
that RoPE is useful because it helps to decay token dependency as relative
distance increases [...] we argue that this is unlikely to be the core
reason." Studying Gemma 7B, they found it builds positional patterns from the
**highest** frequencies and leans hardest on the **lowest** — bands so slow
they barely rotate, leaving them free for meaning.

The mechanism is easy to tell backwards. The decay comes from *dephasing*: at
distance 250 the fastest band has turned 250 radians and cancels, while band
63 has turned 0.0 and adds coherently. **The fast clocks make the bound fall**
— so the frequencies Gemma leans on are the ones responsible for whatever
decay exists.

None of this is a refutation; the same paper argues *for* RoPE elsewhere.
What survives is narrower: the decay is real as a bound, absent as a typical
behaviour, and the frequencies a model leans on are not the ones the original
argument predicts. RoPE is a spectrum, and section 3 and this one argue over
which parts to use.

## 5. Chat This Over With Friends

The short version is that RoPE stores position as an *angle*: rather than
adding a position vector to the embedding it rotates the query and the key,
chosen so that in the dot product the absolute positions cancel and only the
distance survives. The image worth carrying away is that one attention head
runs 64 clocks at once at geometrically spaced speeds — the fastest turns
fully every 6.3 tokens, the slowest needs 54,410. Against a 4,096-token
training window, 18 of those 64 never complete a single turn, which is why a
model falls apart past its trained context and why every extension method is a
decision about which clocks to slow down.

The claim to be careful with is the one nearly every explanation repeats: that
RoPE works because attention decays with distance. The paper does say it, but
the decay belongs to an *upper bound*, and the typical logit is flat — its
variance works out to $d$ however far apart the tokens sit. A 2024 study of
Gemma argues decay is "unlikely to be the core reason" RoPE helps, finding the
model leaning hardest on the slowest bands, which contribute no decay at all.
That is one paper on one model family, so the honest version stays narrow:
decay is real as a bound, absent as a typical behaviour, and whether a model
uses it is the model's own choice. Which is more interesting than the myth it
replaces.
