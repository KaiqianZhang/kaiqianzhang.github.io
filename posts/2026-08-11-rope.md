Attention has no built-in sense of order. As an operation it is *permutation
equivariant*: shuffle the tokens and the outputs shuffle with them, unchanged.
If you want a model to know that "dog bites man" differs from "man bites dog",
you have to arrange for it.

The obvious arrangement is to give each position a tag — a vector meaning "I
am token 5" — and add it to the embedding. That is what the original
Transformer did. **Rotary position embedding**, or **RoPE**, does something
stranger. It adds nothing. It takes the query and key vectors and *turns*
them, by an angle proportional to position.

Almost every open-weight language model of the last three years uses it. This
post builds the mechanism from scratch, shows it moving, and then follows it
to where the argument currently is — which is not about whether to rotate, but
about how fast, and about whether the reason the method was justified by is
the reason it works.

## A Rotation That Is Thrown Away

In its original formulation RoPE is applied **inside each attention head**, to
the queries and keys only, just before the dot product. It never touches the
values and never touches the residual stream: the rotation happens to a copy,
is used once, and is gone. That already distinguishes it from an additive tag,
which travels with the representation through the whole network.

"Original formulation" is doing real work there. GPT-J rotates a quarter of
each head's dimensions; DeepSeek's latent attention carries RoPE on a small
dedicated subset; Llama 4 applies it in three layers out of four. The clean
description is a starting point, not a description of the field.

RoPE also has **no learned parameters** — the angles come from a formula. That
too has eroded; section 5 is the story of how.

One distinction inside the first box matters later. Past the training length a
learned table has no row for position 5000, while sinusoids are defined at
every integer, so the model runs and merely degrades. Extrapolation was
Vaswani et al.'s stated reason for preferring sinusoids, and it did not really
work.

The 2021 question is the sharp one: is there a function $f$ writing *absolute*
position into each vector such that the dot product depends only on the
*relative* one? If so, you get relative behaviour with no relative machinery.
It exists, and section 2 derives it.

**Adoption, stated carefully.** Among dense open-weight decoder-only models
released since 2023 — LLaMA, Mistral, Qwen, Gemma, DeepSeek, Phi, OLMo — RoPE
is close to universal. It is not universal in general: BLOOM and MPT use
ALiBi, Jamba uses nothing, the Mamba and RWKV families are not attention
models at all, and nobody outside the labs knows what the closed frontier
models do.

## 2. The Angle That Cancels

Start with the smallest case that can possibly work: two dimensions. Nearly
everything interesting about RoPE is already visible there, and the step up to
a full head turns out to be bookkeeping. Let $R(\alpha)$ be rotation by
$\alpha$, and write $q = W_q x_m$ and $k = W_k x_n$ for the ordinary projected
query and key. RoPE defines

$$
q_m = R(m\theta)\,q, \qquad k_n = R(n\theta)\,k .
$$

Now take the dot product, and watch what the two facts that make rotations
special do to it — that $R(\alpha)^\top = R(-\alpha)$, and that rotations
compose by adding their angles:

$$
q_m^\top k_n
= \big(R(m\theta)q\big)^{\!\top} R(n\theta)k
= q^\top R(-m\theta)R(n\theta)\,k
= q^\top R\big((n-m)\theta\big)\,k .
$$

**The absolute positions have cancelled.** What is left depends on $m$ and $n$
only through their difference — exactly, by the group structure of rotation.

### Why a rotation, and not something else

Read the two-dimensional vector as a complex number and give $f$ a magnitude
and a phase of its own, $f(q,m) = R_q(m)\,q\,e^{i\Theta_q(m)}$. Its inner
product contributes $R_q(m)R_k(n)$ to the magnitude and
$\Theta_k(n) - \Theta_q(m)$ to the phase. For both to depend only on $n-m$,
the phase forces $\Theta(m) = m\theta$ for a constant $\theta$ and the
magnitude forces $R_q(m)R_k(n)$ to be constant. Set both radii to 1 and $f$ is
a rotation.

Which is a derivation, not a uniqueness theorem. The magnitude condition pins
down only the *product* of the two radii, and **xPos** takes exactly that
freedom, using $\zeta^{m}$ for queries and $\zeta^{-m}$ for keys on top of the
rotation — product still constant, relative-only property intact, result not a
rotation. Rotation is unique among maps that preserve norm and treat queries
and keys alike: a real constraint, but an assumption rather than a
consequence.

Norm preservation is also what makes RoPE safe: a rotation cannot change
$\|q\|$, so position is injected without touching the scale of the logits. An
additive tag changes both direction and length.

### Beyond two dimensions

A head is 64 or 128 dimensional, so RoPE chops the vector into consecutive
pairs and rotates each at its own rate:

$$
\theta_i = \text{base}^{-2i/d}, \qquad i = 0, 1, \ldots, d/2 - 1,
$$

with $\text{base} = 10{,}000$ originally. Pair $0$ turns one radian per token;
each subsequent pair turns geometrically slower, until the last barely moves.

The cancellation holds pair by pair, so it holds for the whole vector. Each
pair contributes a sinusoid in $(n-m)\theta_i$ with its own amplitude *and
phase*, both set by the contents of $q$ and $k$:[^sincos]

$$
q_m^\top k_n = \sum_{i} \Big[ a_i \cos\big((n-m)\theta_i\big)
                            + b_i \sin\big((n-m)\theta_i\big) \Big].
$$

That the model controls the phases, not merely the amplitudes, matters in
section 6. The chopping into consecutive pairs is a convention: conjugating by
any fixed orthogonal matrix gives an equally valid scheme.

RoPE is not one clock but a bank of them.

## 4. The Version in the Paper and the Version in the Weights

Between the derivation and a running model sits a layer of convention, and two
of these bite.

**The pairing is not the paper's.** Section 2 pairs coordinate $0$ with $1$,
$2$ with $3$, as the paper writes it. The implementation almost everyone runs,
from GPT-NeoX and Hugging Face, pairs $i$ with $i + d/2$ — the `rotate_half`
convention. Equivalent up to a fixed permutation, so the mathematics is
untouched, but weights moved between them without re-permuting are silently
wrong.

**It is cheap, and that is probably why it won.** RoPE is $O(d)$ elementwise
work on $q$ and $k$: a table of cosines and sines, one multiply-add pass. No
per-pair term, nothing inside the attention matrix for a fused kernel to route
around. It composes with FlashAttention and with a key-value cache untouched —
you rotate a key once, when it is written, and never again. Relative-bias
schemes can claim none of that, and the elegance of section 2 is likely a
smaller part of RoPE's victory than this paragraph is.

**The angles are computed in fp32**, above the precision of the activations,
because bfloat16 cannot resolve distinct large positions. And because keys are
cached already rotated, any method that changes the angles after training —
all of section 5 — must recompute the cache or correct what is in it.

           "' fill='none' stroke='#DEDAD4' stroke-width='1.4'/>";
      s += "<line x1='" + cx + "' y1='" + cy + "' x2='" +
           (cx + R * Math.cos(ang)).toFixed(2) + "' y2='" +
           (cy - R * Math.sin(ang)).toFixed(2) + "' stroke='" + colour +
           "' stroke-width='2' stroke-linecap='round'/>";
    }
    scene.innerHTML = s;
    pOut.textContent = 'm = ' + m.toLocaleString();
    bOut.textContent = 'base = ' + Math.round(base).toLocaleString();
    var slowest = Math.pow(base, -2 * (NB - 1) / D);
    note.textContent = never + ' of the 64 bands have not completed a single turn at ' +
      'position ' + m.toLocaleString() + '. The slowest needs ' +
      Math.round(2 * Math.PI / slowest).toLocaleString() + ' tokens to come back round.' +
      (m === 4096 ? '  \u2014 this is Llama 2\u2019s training window.' : '');
  }
  pR.addEventListener('input', draw);
  bR.addEventListener('input', draw);
  draw();
})();
</script>

A model trained with a 4,096-token context has seen every angle each band
produces inside that window and none beyond it. Ask about position 100,000 and
the fast bands are unbothered — they wrap constantly, so a large position is
just another point on a circle. The slow bands are the problem: never having
completed a turn, they are put somewhere genuinely new.

Every extension method is an answer to that picture.

**Position interpolation** (Chen et al., 2023) is the blunt one: to double the
context, halve every position index, so everything lands back inside the range
the model has seen. It works — with about a thousand steps of fine-tuning,
usually dropped when the method is described.

**NTK-aware scaling** changes the base rather than the positions, stretching
slow bands more than fast ones. It appeared in June 2023 as a post by a
pseudonymous user, bloc97, on the LocalLLaMA forum, and was in production
models before it was in any paper.

**NTK-by-parts**, from the same author, is the three-regime split Figure 5
shades: for each band, $r = L/\lambda$ counts its wraps within the context,
and bands with $r > \beta$ are left alone, $r < \alpha$ are interpolated as PI
does, the rest ramped. **YaRN** (Peng et al., 2023) is that plus a temperature
on the logits, correcting an entropy drift that appears when you stretch —
reaching a given context with "10x less tokens and 2.5x less training steps
than previous methods." **LongRoPE** (Ding et al., 2024) drops formulas
altogether, searching per-band factors directly out to 2M tokens.

**Choosing the base up front** is a different act. Extension methods change a
trained model's angles afterwards; CodeLlama and Llama 3 simply pretrained
with a larger base — 500,000 in Llama 3's case — before any weights existed.
Llama 3.1's 128k context still needed a piecewise scheme on top.

All of these are statements about *which clocks to slow down* — a coherent
question only because RoPE built position out of many independent frequencies
in the first place.

## 6. A Bound That Falls, and Nothing Underneath It

The paper's second argument is the one that got repeated everywhere. Setting
$\theta_i = \text{base}^{-2i/d}$ gives, in the authors' words, "a long-term
decay property [...], which means the inner-product will decay when the
relative position increase."

    <img src='/images/rope-long-term-decay.png'

        The paper's evidence. Read the axis label: this is an <i>upper bound</i>
        on the inner product, not the inner product. It falls from about 20 to

A bound that falls does not oblige anything underneath it to fall, and the
check is short because section 2 already gives the closed form. For $q$ and
$k$ with independent standard-normal entries, $\mathbb{E}[a_i^2] =
\mathbb{E}[b_i^2] = 2$ and the cross terms vanish, so

$$
\operatorname{Var}\big(q_m^\top k_n\big)
= \sum_{i} \Big[ 2\cos^2\big((n{-}m)\theta_i\big)
               + 2\sin^2\big((n{-}m)\theta_i\big) \Big]
= 2 \cdot \tfrac{d}{2} = d ,
$$

by the Pythagorean identity, for **every** relative distance. The typical size
of the logit does not decay at all.

Barbero and colleagues reached the same place in 2024 from the other
direction: "A common belief is that RoPE is useful because it helps to decay
token dependency as relative distance increases [...] we argue that this is
unlikely to be the core reason." Studying Gemma 7B, they found the model builds
positional patterns out of the **highest** frequencies and leans most heavily
on the **lowest** — bands so slow they barely rotate, leaving them as nearly
position-independent channels for semantics.

The mechanism is easy to tell backwards. The decay in Figure 6 comes from
*dephasing*: at distance 250 the fastest band has turned 250 radians and its
contributions cancel, while band 63 has turned 0.0 radians and adds
coherently. The sixteen fastest bands together give a magnitude of 2.0 out of
a possible 16; all 64 give 22.2. **The fast clocks are what makes the bound
fall** — so the frequencies Gemma leans on for position are exactly the ones
responsible for whatever decay exists.

None of this is a refutation; the same paper argues *for* RoPE elsewhere,
showing that a head with no positional encoding cannot construct certain
diagonal patterns a RoPE head can.

What survives is narrower than a debunking. The decay property is real as a
bound and absent as a typical behaviour, and the frequencies a model leans on
are not the ones the original argument would predict. RoPE stopped being a
fixed formula some time ago: it is a spectrum, and both the engineering of
section 5 and the interpretability of this one are arguments about which parts
of it to use.

## 7. Chat This Over With Friends

The short version is that RoPE stores position as an *angle*. Rather than
adding a position vector to the embedding, it rotates the query and the key,
and it chooses the rotation so that when you take their dot product the two
absolute positions cancel and only the distance between them survives —
exactly, by the algebra of rotations, not approximately and not only for large
models. The image worth carrying away is that a single attention head is
running 64 clocks at once at geometrically spaced speeds: the fastest
completes a full turn every 6.3 tokens, the slowest needs 54,410. Against a
4,096-token training window, 18 of those 64 clocks never complete a single
turn, which is precisely why a model falls apart past its trained context —
and why every extension method, from position interpolation through YaRN, is
really a decision about which clocks to slow down. Llama 3 changed one number,
the base, from 10,000 to 500,000, and stretched the slowest clock out to 2.6
million tokens.

The claim to be careful with is the one nearly every explanation repeats: that
RoPE works because attention decays with distance. The paper does say it, but
the decay belongs to an *upper bound*, and the typical logit is flat in
distance — its variance works out to $d$ however far apart the two tokens sit.
A 2024 study of Gemma argues decay is "unlikely to be the core reason" RoPE
helps, and finds the model leaning hardest on the slowest bands: the ones
contributing no decay at all, behaving instead like nearly position-independent
channels that can carry meaning. That is one paper on one model family, and it
argues *for* RoPE elsewhere, so the honest version stays narrow — decay is
real as a bound, absent as a typical behaviour, and whether a model uses it is
the model's own choice. Which is a considerably more interesting fact than the
myth it replaces.
