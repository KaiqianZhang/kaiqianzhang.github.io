RoPE

Position becomes an angle, the angle cancels, and what is left is a bank of clocks that the whole field is now busy retuning.

Published

11 August 2026

Read time

17 min read

Tags

Foundations for LLM

Attention has no built-in sense of order. As an operation it is permutation equivariant: shuffle the tokens and the outputs shuffle with them, unchanged. If you want a model to know that "dog bites man" differs from "man bites dog", you have to arrange for it.

The obvious arrangement is to give each position a tag — a vector meaning "I am token 5" — and add it to the embedding. That is what the original Transformer did. Rotary position embedding, or RoPE, does something stranger. It adds nothing. It takes the query and key vectors and turns them, by an angle proportional to position.

Almost every open-weight language model of the last three years uses it. This post builds the mechanism from scratch, shows it moving, and then follows it to where the argument currently is — which is not about whether to rotate, but about how fast, and about whether the reason the method was justified by is the reason it works.

A Rotation That Is Thrown Away

In its original formulation RoPE is applied inside each attention head, to the queries and keys only, just before the dot product. It never touches the values and never touches the residual stream: the rotation happens to a copy, is used once, and is gone. That already distinguishes it from an additive tag, which travels with the representation through the whole network.

"Original formulation" is doing real work there. GPT-J rotates a quarter of each head's dimensions; DeepSeek's latent attention carries RoPE on a small dedicated subset; Llama 4 applies it in three layers out of four. The clean description is a starting point, not a description of the field.

RoPE also has no learned parameters — the angles come from a formula. That too has eroded; section 5 is the story of how.

1. Four Answers, and One of Them Is Silence

Four ways to tell a model where a token sits, in the order tried. The labels above the arrows are what each answer was still paying.

One distinction inside the first box matters later. Past the training length a learned table has no row for position 5000, while sinusoids are defined at every integer, so the model runs and merely degrades. Extrapolation was Vaswani et al.'s stated reason for preferring sinusoids, and it did not really work.

The 2021 question is the sharp one: is there a function $f$ writing absolute position into each vector such that the dot product depends only on the relative one? If so, you get relative behaviour with no relative machinery. It exists, and section 2 derives it.

Adoption, stated carefully. Among dense open-weight decoder-only models released since 2023 — LLaMA, Mistral, Qwen, Gemma, DeepSeek, Phi, OLMo — RoPE is close to universal. It is not universal in general: BLOOM and MPT use ALiBi, Jamba uses nothing, the Mamba and RWKV families are not attention models at all, and nobody outside the labs knows what the closed frontier models do.

2. The Angle That Cancels

Start with the smallest case that can possibly work: two dimensions. Nearly everything interesting about RoPE is already visible there, and the step up to a full head turns out to be bookkeeping. Let $R(\alpha)$ be rotation by $\alpha$, and write $q = W_q x_m$ and $k = W_k x_n$ for the ordinary projected query and key. RoPE defines

$$ q_m = R(m\theta)\,q, \qquad k_n = R(n\theta)\,k . $$

Now take the dot product, and watch what the two facts that make rotations special do to it — that $R(\alpha)^\top = R(-\alpha)$, and that rotations compose by adding their angles:

$$ q_m^\top k_n = \big(R(m\theta)q\big)^{\!\top} R(n\theta)k = q^\top R(-m\theta)R(n\theta)\,k = q^\top R\big((n-m)\theta\big)\,k . $$

The absolute positions have cancelled. What is left depends on $m$ and $n$ only through their difference — exactly, by the group structure of rotation.

Why a rotation, and not something else

Read the two-dimensional vector as a complex number and give $f$ a magnitude and a phase of its own, $f(q,m) = R_q(m)\,q\,e^{i\Theta_q(m)}$. Its inner product contributes $R_q(m)R_k(n)$ to the magnitude and $\Theta_k(n) - \Theta_q(m)$ to the phase. For both to depend only on $n-m$, the phase forces $\Theta(m) = m\theta$ for a constant $\theta$ and the magnitude forces $R_q(m)R_k(n)$ to be constant. Set both radii to 1 and $f$ is a rotation.

Which is a derivation, not a uniqueness theorem. The magnitude condition pins down only the product of the two radii, and xPos takes exactly that freedom, using $\zeta^{m}$ for queries and $\zeta^{-m}$ for keys on top of the rotation — product still constant, relative-only property intact, result not a rotation. Rotation is unique among maps that preserve norm and treat queries and keys alike: a real constraint, but an assumption rather than a consequence.

Norm preservation is also what makes RoPE safe: a rotation cannot change $\|q\|$, so position is injected without touching the scale of the logits. An additive tag changes both direction and length.

Beyond two dimensions

A head is 64 or 128 dimensional, so RoPE chops the vector into consecutive pairs and rotates each at its own rate:

$$ \theta_i = \text{base}^{-2i/d}, \qquad i = 0, 1, \ldots, d/2 - 1, $$

with $\text{base} = 10{,}000$ originally. Pair $0$ turns one radian per token; each subsequent pair turns geometrically slower, until the last barely moves.

The cancellation holds pair by pair, so it holds for the whole vector. Each pair contributes a sinusoid in $(n-m)\theta_i$ with its own amplitude and phase, both set by the contents of $q$ and $k$:1

$$ q_m^\top k_n = \sum_{i} \Big[ a_i \cos\big((n-m)\theta_i\big) + b_i \sin\big((n-m)\theta_i\big) \Big]. $$

That the model controls the phases, not merely the amplitudes, matters in section 6. The chopping into consecutive pairs is a convention: conjugating by any fixed orthogonal matrix gives an equally valid scheme.

RoPE is not one clock but a bank of them.

3. Watching the Gap Refuse to Move

Four lines of algebra is exactly the length at which something can be true without being felt. So here it is moving.

(a) Position becomes angle. Read two coordinates of a token's vector as a point on a dial. RoPE turns it by an angle proportional to position: one step forward, one turn of $\theta$.
(b) Only the gap survives. Slide the whole sentence one token later and both hands turn together. The shaded gap between them — fixed by how far apart the two tokens are — never changes. The dot product sees only that gap.
Figure 1. Absolute position moves; relative position does not. Watch the wedge in (b) rather than the arrows — the arrows do all the visible work and none of the important work. This is one frequency band of one head; a real head runs $d/2$ dials at once, at speeds four orders of magnitude apart.

Figure 2. The same statement as Figure 1, drawn along the sequence rather than on a dial. Position runs into the page and one coordinate pair turns steadily as it goes, so a token's vector traces a helix. The query and the key are two beads on that helix. Slide them together with the first control and the shaded wedge between them never changes; change their separation with the second and it opens. The dot product sees the wedge, and the wedge is a function of the gap alone.

Made concrete: rotate a fixed random $(q, k)$ to every pair of positions in a 128-token window and plot the logit.

Figure 3. The logit for one fixed $(q, k)$ pair at every combination of positions, constant along every diagonal — a diagonal being a set of pairs sharing the same $n - m$. A demonstration rather than a test: section 2 proves it as an identity. What it adds is one caveat. In float64 the variation along a diagonal is under $10^{-14}$, against logits spanning about three units; in bfloat16, which inference actually uses, it is $1.2\times10^{-1}$. The cancellation is exact in the algebra, not in the arithmetic.

4. The Version in the Paper and the Version in the Weights

Between the derivation and a running model sits a layer of convention, and two of these bite.

The pairing is not the paper's. Section 2 pairs coordinate $0$ with $1$, $2$ with $3$, as the paper writes it. The implementation almost everyone runs, from GPT-NeoX and Hugging Face, pairs $i$ with $i + d/2$ — the rotate_half convention. Equivalent up to a fixed permutation, so the mathematics is untouched, but weights moved between them without re-permuting are silently wrong.

It is cheap, and that is probably why it won. RoPE is $O(d)$ elementwise work on $q$ and $k$: a table of cosines and sines, one multiply-add pass. No per-pair term, nothing inside the attention matrix for a fused kernel to route around. It composes with FlashAttention and with a key-value cache untouched — you rotate a key once, when it is written, and never again. Relative-bias schemes can claim none of that, and the elegance of section 2 is likely a smaller part of RoPE's victory than this paragraph is.

The angles are computed in fp32, above the precision of the activations, because bfloat16 cannot resolve distinct large positions. And because keys are cached already rotated, any method that changes the angles after training — all of section 5 — must recompute the cache or correct what is in it.

5. Stretching a Context It Was Never Given

Figure 4. All 64 dials of one head at once, fastest at the top left. Drag the position and the fast bands blur while the slow ones in the bottom rows barely stir — that difference is the whole of section 5. A dial drawn in rose has never completed a full turn at this position, which means the model has seen nothing like this angle during training. Now raise the base: the dials slow down together, and the rose ones are what you are buying more of.

A model trained with a 4,096-token context has seen every angle each band produces inside that window and none beyond it. Ask about position 100,000 and the fast bands are unbothered — they wrap constantly, so a large position is just another point on a circle. The slow bands are the problem: never having completed a turn, they are put somewhere genuinely new.

Figure 5. How many tokens each band needs to return to where it started. The fastest wraps every $2\pi \approx 6.3$ tokens; with the original base of $10^4$ the slowest needs 54,410, so against a 4,096-token context 18 of the 64 bands never complete a turn. A larger base of $5\times10^5$ (plum) stretches the slowest to 2.6 million. Grey shading marks the three NTK-by-parts regimes and relates to neither curve. Generated for this post.

Every extension method is an answer to that picture.

Position interpolation (Chen et al., 2023) is the blunt one: to double the context, halve every position index, so everything lands back inside the range the model has seen. It works — with about a thousand steps of fine-tuning, usually dropped when the method is described.

NTK-aware scaling changes the base rather than the positions, stretching slow bands more than fast ones. It appeared in June 2023 as a post by a pseudonymous user, bloc97, on the LocalLLaMA forum, and was in production models before it was in any paper.

NTK-by-parts, from the same author, is the three-regime split Figure 5 shades: for each band, $r = L/\lambda$ counts its wraps within the context, and bands with $r > \beta$ are left alone, $r < \alpha$ are interpolated as PI does, the rest ramped. YaRN (Peng et al., 2023) is that plus a temperature on the logits, correcting an entropy drift that appears when you stretch — reaching a given context with "10x less tokens and 2.5x less training steps than previous methods." LongRoPE (Ding et al., 2024) drops formulas altogether, searching per-band factors directly out to 2M tokens.

Choosing the base up front is a different act. Extension methods change a trained model's angles afterwards; CodeLlama and Llama 3 simply pretrained with a larger base — 500,000 in Llama 3's case — before any weights existed. Llama 3.1's 128k context still needed a piecewise scheme on top.

All of these are statements about which clocks to slow down — a coherent question only because RoPE built position out of many independent frequencies in the first place.

6. A Bound That Falls, and Nothing Underneath It

The paper's second argument is the one that got repeated everywhere. Setting $\theta_i = \text{base}^{-2i/d}$ gives, in the authors' words, "a long-term decay property [...], which means the inner-product will decay when the relative position increase."

Figure 6. The paper's evidence. Read the axis label: this is an upper bound on the inner product, not the inner product. It falls from about 20 to roughly 7 across 250 tokens, oscillating the whole way.
Figure 2, Su et al. (2021), recoloured.

A bound that falls does not oblige anything underneath it to fall, and the check is short because section 2 already gives the closed form. For $q$ and $k$ with independent standard-normal entries, $\mathbb{E}[a_i^2] = \mathbb{E}[b_i^2] = 2$ and the cross terms vanish, so

$$ \operatorname{Var}\big(q_m^\top k_n\big) = \sum_{i} \Big[ 2\cos^2\big((n{-}m)\theta_i\big) + 2\sin^2\big((n{-}m)\theta_i\big) \Big] = 2 \cdot \tfrac{d}{2} = d , $$

by the Pythagorean identity, for every relative distance. The typical size of the logit does not decay at all.

Figure 7. Both quantities over the range the paper plots, each divided by its value at distance 10. The bound (lavender) falls to 0.37. The realised logit magnitude for random $q, k$ (blue) sits at 0.98 — flat, as the variance calculation says it must, at $\sqrt{d} = 11.31$ throughout. Decay is not something the mechanism imposes. It is something a model can arrange, if it wants it.

Figure 8. Where the decay actually comes from. With all 64 bands summed the curve is flat: the typical logit does not fall with distance, which is the calculation above. Now pull the first slider down. Keeping only the fastest bands produces a steep, oscillating decay — because fast bands dephase, and dephasing is what makes a sum shrink. The decay in the paper's bound is manufactured by exactly the bands Gemma turns out to use for position, and the slow bands that contribute none of it are the ones it appears to have repurposed for meaning.

Barbero and colleagues reached the same place in 2024 from the other direction: "A common belief is that RoPE is useful because it helps to decay token dependency as relative distance increases [...] we argue that this is unlikely to be the core reason." Studying Gemma 7B, they found the model builds positional patterns out of the highest frequencies and leans most heavily on the lowest — bands so slow they barely rotate, leaving them as nearly position-independent channels for semantics.

The mechanism is easy to tell backwards. The decay in Figure 6 comes from dephasing: at distance 250 the fastest band has turned 250 radians and its contributions cancel, while band 63 has turned 0.0 radians and adds coherently. The sixteen fastest bands together give a magnitude of 2.0 out of a possible 16; all 64 give 22.2. The fast clocks are what makes the bound fall — so the frequencies Gemma leans on for position are exactly the ones responsible for whatever decay exists.

None of this is a refutation; the same paper argues for RoPE elsewhere, showing that a head with no positional encoding cannot construct certain diagonal patterns a RoPE head can.

What survives is narrower than a debunking. The decay property is real as a bound and absent as a typical behaviour, and the frequencies a model leans on are not the ones the original argument would predict. RoPE stopped being a fixed formula some time ago: it is a spectrum, and both the engineering of section 5 and the interpretability of this one are arguments about which parts of it to use.

7. Chat This Over With Friends

The one-line version. RoPE encodes position by rotating a token's query and key rather than adding anything to them, chosen so that in the dot product the absolute positions cancel exactly, leaving only the distance between the two tokens.

The detail that lands. A single head runs 64 clocks at once, at geometrically spaced speeds. The fastest turns fully every 6.3 tokens; the slowest needs 54,410. Against a 4,096-token training window, 18 of those 64 clocks never complete a single turn — which is why models break past their trained context, and why every extension method is really a decision about which clocks to slow down. Llama 3 changed one number, the base, from 10,000 to 500,000, stretching the slowest clock to 2.6 million tokens.

What most people get wrong. Nearly every explanation says RoPE works because attention decays with distance. The paper does claim it — but that decay is a property of an upper bound, and for random queries and keys the realised logit size is exactly flat in distance, since the variance works out to $d$ regardless of separation. A 2024 study of Gemma argues decay is "unlikely to be the core reason" RoPE helps.

If someone pushes back. Fairly — that is one paper on one model family, and it argues for RoPE elsewhere. What survives is narrower: decay is real as a bound, absent as a typical behaviour, and whether a model uses it is the model's choice.

The bit that surprises people. RoPE has no learned parameters at all. The angles are fixed by formula before training starts, and the model's only freedom is which of them to lean on.
